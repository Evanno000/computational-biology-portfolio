01 / OVERVIEW

Project overview.

Protein sequence analysis is an important component of bioinformatics. A protein's amino acid sequence contains information that can be used to calculate physicochemical properties and investigate patterns in its composition.

In this project, the canonical human serum albumin precursor sequence was obtained from UniProt and analysed using a Python-based computational pipeline.

The pipeline validates the sequence, calculates physicochemical properties, measures amino acid composition, performs sliding-window hydropathy analysis and generates visualizations and scientific reports.

02 / OBJECTIVES

Analysis objectives.

  • Retrieve and validate a protein sequence using Biopython.
  • Calculate sequence length and molecular weight.
  • Calculate the theoretical isoelectric point.
  • Determine the amino acid composition and percentages.
  • Calculate the GRAVY hydropathicity score.
  • Perform sliding-window hydropathy analysis.
  • Generate scientific visualizations and structured outputs.
  • Produce automated scientific interpretation and reports.
03 / METHODOLOGY

Computational workflow.

01. Sequence retrieval and validation

The human serum albumin sequence was retrieved from UniProt in FASTA format. Biopython was used to load the sequence and validate its amino acid characters before downstream calculations.

02. Physicochemical properties

The analysis calculated sequence length, molecular weight, theoretical isoelectric point and GRAVY.

03. Amino acid composition

The frequency of each of the 20 standard amino acids was calculated. Counts and percentages were exported as a CSV dataset and used to generate a composition plot.

04. Sliding-window hydropathy analysis

The Kyte–Doolittle hydropathy scale was used to investigate local hydropathic patterns. A window size of 21 amino acids was applied across the sequence. Each window was evaluated to calculate its mean hydropathy score.

05. Automated reporting

The pipeline generated a summary of calculated properties, composition results, hydropathy findings and scientific limitations.

08 / INTERPRETATION

Scientific interpretation.

Overall hydropathicity

The negative GRAVY score indicates that the average amino acid composition is relatively hydrophilic according to the Kyte–Doolittle scale. This is a sequence-derived characterization and does not independently establish experimental solubility.

Local hydropathic patterns

The sliding-window profile identifies regions with differing local hydropathy scores. The highest and lowest values represent the most hydrophobic and hydrophilic windows, respectively, within this calculated profile.

Theoretical isoelectric point

The calculated theoretical pI is 5.92. This estimate describes the pH at which the protein is predicted to have approximately zero net charge under the calculation model.

Scientific context: The analyzed FASTA sequence represents the canonical precursor. Mature albumin undergoes biological processing, so the precursor sequence should be distinguished from the mature protein when interpreting biological results.

09 / LIMITATIONS

Scientific limitations.

  • The analysis uses sequence-derived calculations rather than experimentally measured protein properties.
  • Theoretical pI and molecular weight are computational estimates.
  • Hydropathy profiles depend on the selected hydropathy scale and window size.
  • Hydropathy patterns alone cannot establish protein folding or structural organization.
  • The analysis does not establish membrane topology or biological activity.
  • The canonical precursor sequence undergoes biological processing before mature albumin is produced.
